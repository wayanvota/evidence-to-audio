# Editorial method

The workflow is designed around one measure: value per minute of listener attention.

## Selection standard

An idea deserves audio time when it is:

- New enough to matter now.
- Supported well enough to explain without hiding uncertainty.
- Consequential enough to change a decision, experiment, or mental model.
- Distinct from the other selected ideas.
- Understandable without a visual appendix.

A famous source receives no automatic preference. A minor source can lead. A week with one strong idea should produce a short episode.

## Claim discipline

The agents should keep four categories separate:

1. What a source directly observed.
2. How the source's author interpreted the observation.
3. What a vendor or institution claims about itself.
4. What the episode infers for the listener.

The spoken script should preserve this separation through attribution and careful transitions.

## The Critic's test

For the proposed episode, the Critic names:

- The strongest claim.
- The weakest joint.
- The missing evidence.
- The likely counterargument.
- The revision that makes the argument harder to dismiss.

If the editor cannot make those limits audible without overwhelming the idea, the material may belong in show notes rather than the episode.

## Attention test

After listening, record where attention dropped, where the host repeated a point, which caveat was hard to follow, and what action remained memorable. Revise the prompts from observed listening failures rather than adding generic production flourishes.
